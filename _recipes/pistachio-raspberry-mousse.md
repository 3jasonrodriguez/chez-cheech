## Ingredients

- 1.5 cups graham crackers, crushed to fine crumbs
- 6 tbsp unsalted butter, melted
- 2 tbsp granulated sugar (for crust)
- 9.95 oz jar raspberry fruit spread (full jar)
- 2 tbsp water (or lemon juice, to loosen raspberry)
- 2.5 tsp powdered gelatin (for mousse)
- 4 tbsp cold water (to bloom gelatin, mousse)
- 2.5 cups heavy cream, divided
- 1.25 cups pistachio cream spread (Pisti)
- 2.5 tbsp granulated sugar (for mousse, adjust to taste)
- 1/4 cup crushed pistachios, for topping

## Steps

1. Mix graham cracker crumbs, melted butter, and sugar until it looks like wet sand. Press firmly into the bottom of an 8-9 inch springform pan. Chill in the freezer while you prep the rest.
2. Sprinkle gelatin over cold water in a small bowl. Let sit 5 minutes to bloom.
3. Gently warm 3/4 cup heavy cream in a small saucepan (don't boil). Off heat, whisk in the bloomed gelatin until dissolved, then whisk in pistachio spread until smooth. Let cool to room temperature, stirring occasionally.
4. In a separate bowl, whip the remaining 1.75 cups heavy cream with sugar to soft-medium peaks.
5. Fold the cooled pistachio mixture into the whipped cream in two additions, gently, until no streaks remain.
6. Pour the mousse over the chilled crust, smoothing the top. Chill in the fridge at least 4 hours, ideally overnight, until fully set.
7. Warm the raspberry spread with water (or lemon juice) in a small saucepan, simmering briefly until loosened and pourable. Strain if you want it smooth. Cool slightly.
8. Run a thin knife around the edge, release the springform, top with crushed pistachios, slice, and serve with raspberry sauce drizzled over each piece.

## Changelog

- **2026-07-26** — Made with raspberry as a gelled layer (crust → raspberry → mousse), full 9.95oz jar split into two gelatin batches. Layer came out too solid — too much gelatin for the texture I wanted. Pistachio increased to 1.25 cups for stronger flavor. Result: 4.5 stars. Mousse texture and taste were excellent. Next time: try raspberry as a warm stovetop sauce instead (no gelatin, just spread + water or lemon juice, simmered and optionally strained) — drizzle over each slice at serving rather than baking it into a layer.
